# Connect Loomio with meetings and other tools

Use Loomio to make meetings more prepared and decisions easier to revisit. Keep live conversation for work that benefits from people being together.

## Before a meeting

Use Loomio to:

- collect agenda items in a discussion;
- use a Choose, Score, or Allocate poll to identify priorities;
- find a meeting time with a [Time poll](/en/user_manual/polls/meeting_polls);
- share background information before the meeting;
- run a Question round so the meeting does not begin with basic clarification;
- use a Sense check to find concerns that need live attention; and
- complete straightforward consent decisions that do not need meeting time.

State what preparation is expected and by when. A long thread without a clear purpose can create as much work as a long meeting.

## During a meeting

Meet when the group needs real-time listening, relationship repair, complex proposal forming, or careful integration of an objection. Loomio does not replace facilitation.

Keep the relevant Loomio discussion open during the meeting. Record material changes to the proposal and confirm that people responding remotely are considering the same version as people in the room.

## After a meeting

Return the result to Loomio:

- update the discussion context with the current proposal or a concise summary;
- run the agreed Consent proposal if the meeting prepared but did not make the decision;
- publish the outcome if consent was reached in the meeting;
- name the people responsible for next steps; and
- set a review date when the agreement is experimental or time-bound.

This closes the loop for people who could not attend and keeps the decision from disappearing into private notes.

## Give each tool a clear job

Sociocratic organizations commonly use several tools. [Sociocracy For All notes](https://www.sociocracyforall.org/implementation/) that no single standard toolset fits every implementation.

A workable division is:

| Tool | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Loomio discussions | Working documents, focused discussion, proposals, consent, outcomes, and decision history |
| Loomio Files | Legal documents, signed records, policies, and other source files |
| Chat | Brief coordination, social conversation, and links back to Loomio |
| External document editor | Specialized drafting or review features the group cannot do in Loomio |
| Task system | Assignments, priorities, dependencies, and delivery tracking |
| Circle-and-role directory | Current aims, domains, roles, holders, links, and terms |
| Video or in-person meeting | High-bandwidth dialogue, facilitation, and relationship work |

Post a Loomio link in chat when a conversation becomes a governance matter. Do not try to finish the decision in the chat stream as well. Maintaining two decision records creates uncertainty about which one is authoritative.

## Keep participation manageable

People are more likely to participate when they can see exactly what is being asked of them.

- Use one discussion for one decision or a closely related sequence.
- Put the decision question near the top.
- Summarize long discussions and say what changed.
- Use a deadline and closing reminder.
- Mention people whose input is specifically needed.
- Invite only the circle or affected people rather than notifying the whole organization by default.
- Move an issue to a meeting when written discussion is increasing conflict or losing important context.

If participation drops, investigate the process as well as the software. Check whether the decision matters, the responsible circle is clear, the request is understandable, members have enough time, and facilitators are following through on outcomes.
