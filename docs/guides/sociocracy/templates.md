# Templates for sociocratic work

Copy these prompts into Loomio, then adapt the language and settings to your governance agreement. Delete instructions that do not apply. A shorter template that people understand is better than a comprehensive template they do not read.

Group administrators can turn recurring prompts into [poll templates](/en/user_manual/polls/poll_templates). Test a new template as a draft before making it part of the circle's normal process.

## Circle description

```text
Aim
[What effect or value does this circle exist to create?]

Domain
[What work, resources, and policy decisions does this circle have authority over?]

Membership and links
[Who participates? Who links this circle with another circle? Link to the current role record.]

How we decide
[Which decisions use consent? Which decisions are delegated to roles? What counts as an objection? What participation is required?]

Working rhythm
[Meeting schedule, expected response time, and how to request facilitation.]

Current records
[Links to editable governance documents, the role directory, and source files in the group's Files tab.]
```

## Decision discussion

Use this context to open one policy question or other consequential decision.

```text
Decision needed
[State the decision as one clear question.]

Why this belongs to this circle
[Name the relevant part of the circle's domain.]

Current situation
[What is happening now? Include evidence and links, not every historical detail.]

Draft proposal
[State the action or policy, its scope, and when it would begin.]

Constraints and safeguards
[Budget, legal requirements, boundaries, dependencies, or risks already addressed.]

Who is affected or should be consulted
[Name roles, circles, or people.]

Process
[Questions until date; reactions until date; consent until date. State who facilitates.]

Review
[How and when will the circle know whether the decision is working?]
```

## Clarifying question round

Use a Question round when people need shared understanding before giving reactions.

**Title:** `Questions: [decision or proposal]`

**Prompt:**

```text
What do you need to know to understand the proposal?

Ask for information before offering an opinion or solution. The proposer will answer here or update the linked material. This round closes [date and time].
```

Close the round with a short outcome listing answers that changed the proposal or revealed missing information.

## Reaction round

Use a Sense check to improve a draft before asking for consent.

**Title:** `Reactions: [draft proposal]`

**Details:**

```text
Please respond to this draft before [date and time].

In your reason, say:
- what supports the circle's aim;
- what concern, need, or information is missing; and
- what change you suggest, if any.

This is not the consent round. The proposer will summarize the input and publish the amended proposal before consent is tested.
```

## Consent proposal

**Title:** `Consent: [specific action or policy]`

**Details:**

```text
Proposal
[State the complete proposal. Link to a longer controlled document if necessary.]

Aim and domain
[Explain why the proposal belongs to this circle and how it serves the aim.]

Changes since the reaction round
[Summarize material amendments.]

Implementation
[Who will do what, by when?]

Safeguards and limits
[State the boundaries that make the proposal safe enough to try.]

Review
[Date, measures, and who will bring the review back to the circle.]

Respond by [date and time]. Select Consent if this is safe enough to try. Select Objection only when you can describe a material risk to the circle's aim or ability to do its work.
```

Configure the response options to match the circle's agreement. A common pair is:

- **Consent** — `I know of no reason this proposal would impair the circle's aim. It is safe enough to try.`
- **Objection** — `I can describe a material risk this proposal must address before the circle proceeds.`

Require a reason for an objection. Do not add a neutral option unless the governance agreement defines what it means and how it affects the result.

Before opening the proposal, check:

- the eligible voters are the people entitled to decide;
- the closing time works across members' schedules;
- reminders are enabled;
- quorum is used only if the governance agreement defines one;
- result visibility supports the process; and
- anonymous voting is off for a normal consent process, so objections can be discussed and responses can be changed.

## Responding to an objection

The facilitator can reply with:

```text
Thank you. I understand the risk as: [brief neutral restatement].

Which part of the circle's aim or work could this impair?
What evidence or experience makes the risk material?
What amendment, safeguard, smaller scope, or shorter trial would address it?

The proposal remains open while we test and integrate this objection. If we cannot do that clearly in writing, [facilitator] will arrange a meeting and bring the result back here.
```

Do not ask the objector to solve the whole proposal alone. The circle is responsible for integrating the information. If the proposal changes materially, notify everyone and invite all participants to reconsider their response.

## Decision outcome

```text
Decision
[State exactly what the circle consented to.]

Reason
[Briefly record the need and why this response was chosen.]

Changes made during the process
[Record safeguards or amendments arising from reactions and objections.]

Responsibility
[Who owns implementation and the next action?]

Current record
[Link to the policy, role record, or other document updated by this decision.]

Review
[Date, measures, and person responsible for bringing the review back.]
```

Publish the [outcome](/en/user_manual/polls/outcomes) even if the proposal is withdrawn or does not reach consent. State what was learned and what will happen next.

## Role selection discussion

```text
Role
[Name of role]

Purpose
[Why the role exists]

Accountabilities and authority
[What the holder is expected and authorized to do]

Term and capacity
[Start, end or review date, and expected time or other constraints]

Selection criteria
[Skills, relationships, experience, or qualities relevant to the role]

Eligible nominees
[Who can hold the role]

Eligible participants
[Who takes part in the selection]

Process and dates
1. Nominate one eligible person and give your reasons by [date].
2. Read every nomination, then ask questions or change your nomination by [date].
3. The facilitator will propose one candidate and explain the reasoning.
4. The candidate may accept or decline.
5. The circle will test the candidate proposal for consent.
```

Keep nominations and reasons visible. The number of nominations is information for the facilitator, not an automatic result.
