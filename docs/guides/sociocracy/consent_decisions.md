# Run consent decisions

Use Loomio to separate the stages of a consent process, give circle members time to participate, and preserve the proposal, objections, amendments, and outcome in one record.

The detailed [Consent process](/en/guides/making_decisions/consent_process) explains the full sequence. This page focuses on the choices a sociocratic circle should make when applying that process in Loomio.

## Confirm the decision belongs to the circle

Before opening a proposal, ask:

- Is this a policy decision within this circle's domain?
- Could a person in an existing role make the decision instead?
- Which people are affected or have useful expertise?
- Does the proposal need questions, reactions, or co-creation before consent?
- What would make the proposal good enough for now and safe enough to try?
- When and how will the circle evaluate the decision?

Not every operational choice needs circle consent. Keeping delegated decisions with the responsible role leaves the circle's attention for policy and other consequential decisions.

## Keep one decision process in one discussion

Start a discussion with the decision to be made, the circle's authority, relevant evidence, and a link to any working document. Use proposals inside that discussion as the process advances:

1. **Question round** when people need shared understanding before reacting.
2. **Sense check** to gather reactions and improve the proposal.
3. Amend the proposal and summarize what changed.
4. **Consent** to ask whether the amended proposal is safe enough to try.
5. Resolve objections and invite people to change their vote, or run another Consent proposal for a materially revised version.
6. Publish an **outcome** recording the decision and what happens next.

Do not force every decision through every stage. A familiar, low-risk proposal may be ready for consent after a short discussion. A complex proposal may need several rounds.

## Configure the Consent template

Group administrators can adapt the [Consent poll template](/en/user_manual/polls/poll_templates) to match the circle's governance agreement.

Check these settings:

- **Consent meaning:** the proposal is within the participant's range of tolerance and they know of no reason it would impair the circle's aim.
- **Objection meaning:** the participant sees a concrete risk to the circle's aim or ability to do its work.
- **Reason requirement:** require a reason for an objection so the concern can be understood and tested.
- **Eligible voters:** invite the members of the circle responsible for the decision.
- **Participation:** set quorum only if your governance agreement uses one. If every circle member must respond, invite exactly those members and follow up with undecided voters rather than treating silence as consent.
- **Duration and reminders:** allow time for different schedules and time zones, and send a closing reminder to undecided voters.
- **Result visibility:** decide whether seeing other responses supports or distorts your process.

Avoid anonymous voting for an ordinary consent process. Anonymous polls do not keep vote reasons with identities and voters cannot change their submitted vote, which prevents the dialogue and revision that objection integration requires.

## Define participation before interpreting the result

Loomio shows who has responded; it does not decide what non-response means. State the circle's rule before opening the proposal. For example:

- every current circle member must respond;
- a defined quorum must respond and no valid objection may remain; or
- named role holders must respond while other members are invited to advise.

If every member must respond, follow up with undecided members. Extend the closing time when someone needs a reasonable opportunity to participate. If a member cannot participate, record how the circle's agreement handles that absence. Never convert silence into consent merely because the poll closed.

## Work with objections

An objection is not a losing vote and should not be treated as a personal veto. It brings information that may show how a proposal would impair the circle's aim. [Sociocracy For All's objection guidance](https://www.sociocracyforall.org/sociocracy-objections/) describes objections as input for improving a proposal.

When someone objects:

1. Ask them to explain the risk and its relationship to the circle's aim.
2. Check that everyone understands the objection before debating a solution.
3. Ask what amendment, safeguard, smaller scope, shorter term, or measurement could address it.
4. Update the proposal clearly and notify the circle of the change.
5. Invite everyone to reconsider their response because the proposal has changed.

The software does not decide whether an objection meets the circle's criteria. The facilitator should test it using the governance agreement and make the reasoning visible. When the objector and proposer disagree, involve the circle or the role authorized to interpret the process; do not resolve the disagreement by counting how many people side with either person.

An objection may reveal that the proposal needs a safeguard, a smaller experiment, more evidence, or a different decision-maker. It may also reveal a concern that should be recorded and reviewed but does not prevent the proposal from proceeding. State which conclusion the circle reached and why.

If an objection cannot be integrated asynchronously, pause the online process and meet. Name the open question and the facilitator before the meeting. Record the resulting amendment and decision back in the discussion so the governance record remains complete.

## Publish the decision and schedule learning

The proposal result is not the decision record by itself. Publish an [outcome](/en/user_manual/polls/outcomes) that states:

- the policy or action consented to;
- safeguards, limits, or measures agreed while resolving objections;
- who is responsible for implementation;
- any unresolved concern the circle wants to observe;
- the review date; and
- where the current policy or role record has been updated.

Review dates make “good enough for now” practical. At the review, compare what happened with the circle's aim and the measures in the decision, then consent to retain, revise, or end the agreement.

## Respect legal decision rules

A group may practise consent while remaining subject to statutes, bylaws, strata rules, or other legal requirements. Decide whether Loomio records the binding decision, prepares a decision that must be ratified elsewhere, or documents input into a formal meeting. Configure the eligible voters, participation requirement, and outcome language accordingly. Seek appropriate professional advice when the legal effect is uncertain.
