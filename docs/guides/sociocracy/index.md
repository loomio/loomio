# Sociocracy on Loomio

Use Loomio to prepare proposals, make consent decisions between meetings, and keep a record of what each circle decided and why.

This guide is for facilitators, circle members, and Loomio administrators. It assumes your organization has already adopted sociocracy, or has a mandate to try it in a defined part of the organization. It does not prescribe one universal version of sociocracy. Use the definitions and decision rules in your own governance agreement.

[Sociocracy For All describes](https://www.sociocracyforall.org/sociocracy/) circles as groups with defined aims and domains that make policy decisions by consent and connect to other circles through linking roles. Loomio can support those practices, especially the work that happens before and after a meeting.

## What this guide helps you do

- decide which circles need their own Loomio subgroup;
- make a circle's aim, domain, membership, and working agreements visible;
- take a proposal through questions, reactions, consent, objections, and outcome;
- select someone for a role without reducing the process to a popularity vote;
- connect asynchronous work with facilitated meetings; and
- establish a dependable practice without creating an empty structure all at once.

## Start with one circle and one real decision

Do not begin by reproducing your entire organization in Loomio. Choose one circle that has a clear mandate, a facilitator, and a current decision to make.

1. Create or identify the circle's Loomio space.
2. Record its aim, domain, membership, and decision rules.
3. Help every member complete one familiar action, such as responding to an introduction or a Time poll.
4. Open one bounded decision that genuinely belongs to the circle.
5. Take it through to a published outcome with an owner and, when useful, a review date.
6. Review the process before adding another circle or more templates.

This sequence gives people a reason to learn the software and exposes unclear governance rules early.

## Decide what belongs in Loomio

Loomio can be the shared home for a circle's governance work. Discussions hold editable working documents, decision context, evidence, questions, reactions, proposals, objections, and outcomes. Keeping these together makes it possible to understand not only what the circle decided, but how the proposal changed and why the outcome was reached.

Group and subgroup descriptions explain the organization and its circles. The members panel shows who participates and the roles they hold. The **Files** tab stores legal documents, signed records, policies, and other source files alongside the discussions that use them.

Task and project management do not belong in Loomio. Use a task system for assignments, priorities, dependencies, status, and delivery, then record any governance decision and its outcome in Loomio. Chat also belongs elsewhere: use it for rapid coordination and social conversation, but move an issue into Loomio when it needs shared context, considered participation, or a durable decision.

Use an external system when a document requires specialized signing, drafting, or regulatory controls that Loomio does not provide. Link the authoritative record to the relevant Loomio discussion rather than maintaining two competing versions.

## Before the first decision

Confirm:

- the circle's aim and domain;
- who is entitled or expected to participate;
- which decisions require circle consent and which are delegated to roles;
- what counts as an objection and how its validity is tested;
- whether every member must respond, a quorum applies, or another participation rule is used;
- who facilitates when an objection cannot be integrated in writing;
- how legal or constitutional decisions must be approved; and
- where the current policy, role, or governance record is maintained.

Do not use a poll setting to answer a governance question the group has not agreed on. Configure Loomio to reflect the process, not to invent it.
