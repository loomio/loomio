# Worked example: consent to a six-week trial

This fictional example follows one proposal from a circle's domain check to its review. It shows how the stages fit together; use your own objection criteria and participation rules.

## Situation

Oatmilk Cooperative's Packaging Circle has this domain:

> Packaging materials, suppliers, return systems, and packaging trials within the approved annual budget.

The circle is considering a six-week returnable-bottle trial. Because the proposal concerns packaging and stays within the approved budget, it belongs to this circle. The Operations role can implement the decision, but cannot adopt the new return system without the circle's consent.

## 1. Open the decision discussion

The proposer starts one discussion titled **Returnable-bottle trial**. The context contains:

- the problem with the current packaging;
- the draft six-week trial;
- costs and the approved budget limit;
- the collection locations;
- the people responsible for logistics;
- links to supplier and hygiene information; and
- a proposed review using return rate, breakage, staff time, and customer feedback.

The proposer names the Packaging Circle's domain so members can check that the decision is in scope before debating the proposal.

## 2. Answer clarifying questions

The facilitator opens a Question round for three working days:

> What do you need to know to understand the returnable-bottle trial?

Members ask who cleans returned bottles, whether deposits are refundable, and how breakage is counted. The proposer answers and updates the draft. Opinions and alternative solutions are held for the reaction round.

## 3. Gather reactions

The facilitator opens a Sense check asking members what supports the circle's aim, what is missing, and what they would change.

The reactions reveal broad support for a trial and two concerns: staff at one collection point have not been consulted, and the draft has no upper limit for additional cleaning time. The proposer consults the staff and amends the draft to include a weekly staffing measure and a stop condition.

The Sense check outcome summarizes those changes. It does not claim that the circle has made a decision.

## 4. Test the amended proposal for consent

The Consent proposal states the whole decision:

> Run the returnable-bottle trial for six weeks at the North and Central collection points, beginning 7 September. Operations owns implementation within the approved trial budget. Record weekly return rate, breakage, additional staff time, and safety incidents. Pause the trial if additional cleaning time exceeds eight hours in any week or if a hygiene incident occurs. Packaging Circle will review the results in the week beginning 19 October.

The proposal is sent only to the members entitled to decide. Every member is expected to respond under this circle's agreement, so the facilitator follows up with undecided members rather than treating silence as consent.

## 5. Integrate an objection

One member objects because the Central collection point has no secure overnight storage. The facilitator first checks the relationship to the circle's aim and the evidence. The concern is material: unsecured returns could contaminate bottles and create extra handling work.

The circle considers several responses. The proposer amends the proposal so Central joins the trial only after Operations confirms a lockable storage area; otherwise the trial proceeds at North alone. The amendment preserves the purpose of the trial while addressing the risk.

Because the proposal changed, all members are notified and invited to reconsider their response. The objector changes their response to Consent after confirming the safeguard.

## 6. Publish the outcome

The facilitator closes the proposal and publishes:

> The Packaging Circle consents to a six-week returnable-bottle trial beginning 7 September. North will participate from the start. Central will participate only after Operations confirms secure overnight storage. Operations owns implementation within the approved budget and will record return rate, breakage, additional staff time, and safety incidents each week. The pause conditions are more than eight additional cleaning hours in one week or any hygiene incident. The circle will review the trial in the week beginning 19 October.

The outcome links to the operating checklist and names the person responsible for scheduling the review.

## 7. Review what happened

At the review date, the circle does not ask whether everyone still likes the idea. It compares the evidence with the trial's purpose and safeguards:

- Did enough bottles return to make the system useful?
- Were breakage and staff time within the agreed bounds?
- Did either pause condition occur?
- What did collection staff and customers learn?
- Should the circle retain, adapt, expand, or end the trial?

The review becomes a new, linked decision discussion. This keeps the original outcome intact while creating a clear record of what the circle learned next.
