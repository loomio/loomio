# Set up circles and roles

Give a circle its own Loomio subgroup when it needs distinct membership, notifications, privacy, or decision history. Do not create a subgroup merely because a circle exists on an organization chart.

## Decide whether the circle needs a subgroup

A subgroup is useful when the circle:

- makes recurring decisions;
- has members who should receive its proposals and reminders;
- needs a private or focused discussion space;
- maintains a decision history separate from the parent circle; or
- needs its own poll templates and permissions.

A temporary project, a role held by one person, or a circle that rarely uses Loomio may be better represented by a tagged discussion in the parent group. Start with the simplest structure that gives the right people a clear place to work.

## Create the circle space

Create one parent group for the organization, then add a [subgroup](/en/user_manual/groups/subgroups) for each circle that needs one.

Use the subgroup description as the circle's front door. Include:

- **Aim:** the effect or value the circle exists to create;
- **Domain:** the matters over which it has authority;
- **Members:** who participates in its governance;
- **Decision rule:** how the circle makes policy decisions and handles non-response;
- **Key roles and links:** where members can find current role holders and role descriptions;
- **Working rhythm:** meeting schedule and expected response time; and
- **Records:** links to editable governance documents and relevant files in the group's **Files** tab.

The [circle description template](/en/guides/sociocracy/templates#circle-description) provides copyable wording.

Choose privacy deliberately. A closed subgroup can be visible to parent-group members and can allow them to read private threads. A secret subgroup is visible only to people invited to it. See [Subgroup settings](/en/user_manual/groups/subgroups#subgroup-settings).

## Make governance work easy to find

Create a `Governance` [category tag](/en/user_manual/discussions/tags) for discussions that change policy, roles, domains, or circle structure. Use more specific tags only when people will actually browse or filter by them.

Pin a short **How this circle works** discussion containing:

- the circle description;
- its current policies, role descriptions, and links to related circle documents;
- its consent and role-selection process;
- how to raise an issue; and
- how to request facilitation.

Update the relevant policy, circle document, or member title when an outcome is published. Link it back to the Loomio outcome so a later reader can see the decision history.

## Use the members panel as a simple role directory

A person's title belongs to their membership in one group, so the same person can have a different title in each circle. Use titles to show current roles in each subgroup's members panel. A title can contain more than one short role name when a person holds several roles in the same circle.

Keep the details of each role—its purpose, accountabilities, authority, and term—in the circle's pinned **How this circle works** discussion. This gives the circle a simple current directory in the members panel and an editable document for the role definitions. When a selection changes a role holder, update the member title and role document as part of publishing the outcome.

Do not treat Loomio permissions as sociocratic authority:

- a **group administrator** can configure the subgroup but does not gain authority over the circle's domain;
- a Loomio **delegate** is someone who can be included in an eligible voting set, not automatically a sociocratic delegate; and
- membership in two groups can support a linking role, but the purpose and accountability of that link still need to be agreed and recorded.

Use [delegated voters](/en/user_manual/groups/delegated_voters) only when that product permission matches the circle's agreed participation rules.

## Select someone for a role

A sociocratic selection is a reasoned nomination and consent process, not a popularity vote. [The Sociocracy For All selection process](https://www.sociocracyforall.org/selection-process/) defines the role and term, gathers nominations with reasons, allows people to change their nomination after hearing the round, proposes a candidate, and tests that proposal for consent.

Run the process in one Loomio discussion:

1. Post the role's purpose, accountabilities, authority, term, selection criteria, and eligible participants.
2. Ask each participant to nominate one eligible person and explain how that person meets the criteria.
3. After all nominations are visible, invite questions and a change round. Anyone changing their nomination should explain what they learned.
4. Have the facilitator name a candidate and explain the reasoning. The candidate can accept or decline.
5. Start a Consent proposal naming that candidate and restating the term.
6. Test and integrate objections using the circle's agreed criteria.
7. Publish an outcome, update the role record and subgroup title, and set the next review or selection date.

The [role selection template](/en/guides/sociocracy/templates#role-selection-discussion) gives prompts for each stage. Use Rank or STV only when the circle's governance agreement calls for that election method; neither method reproduces a sociocratic selection round by itself.
