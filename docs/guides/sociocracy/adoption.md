# Introduce and sustain the practice

Start with one circle and a real need. Let the structure expand after people have experienced a complete decision process and can see why the record matters.

[Sociocracy For All's implementation guidance](https://www.sociocracyforall.org/implementation/) treats adoption as an organizational change process: establish a mandate, design the system, involve affected people, and document the resulting governance agreement. Opening a software account is only one step.

## Prepare the first circle

Before inviting everyone:

1. Confirm that the circle is authorized to use Loomio for its work.
2. Name at least one facilitator and one group administrator.
3. Add the circle's aim, domain, members, and decision rules to the subgroup description.
4. Configure only the discussion and poll templates the circle needs initially.
5. Choose a first meaningful decision with a clear owner and manageable scope.
6. Decide how outcomes will update the governance agreement, policy record, or circle directory.

Keep software administration separate from governance authority. An administrator can configure the group, but that does not give them authority to decide for the circle.

## Use a familiar first action

Begin with a low-stakes action such as:

- an introductory thread where each member responds;
- a Time poll for the next meeting; or
- a simple poll about agenda priorities.

The purpose is to help people sign in, find the circle, read context, respond, and see notifications before the first consequential proposal.

## Move to one real consent decision

Do not remain in demonstration mode. Choose a bounded issue that matters to the circle, explain why it belongs in the circle's domain, and run it through to an outcome.

A useful first decision has:

- enough consequence to justify participation;
- a proposal that can be changed;
- a clear response deadline;
- an identified set of circle members;
- room for a meaningful objection; and
- an outcome the circle can implement and later review.

Expect the first process to reveal gaps. The group may need clearer objection criteria, better reminders, a shorter proposal, or a meeting for part of the work. Treat those findings as improvements to the practice.

## Model constructive objection handling

People may avoid objecting because they do not want to obstruct the group or challenge the proposer. Facilitators can reduce that pressure by explaining that a reasoned objection is information about risk, then responding to the first objection with curiosity rather than defensiveness.

Ask what the objection protects and what change would make the proposal safe enough to try. Record the amendment so everyone can see that raising a concern improved the decision.

## Expand circle by circle

After several complete decisions, review the pilot:

- Did the right people participate?
- Were members able to distinguish preference, concern, and objection?
- Did decisions move faster or become clearer?
- Were outcomes implemented and easy to find?
- Did the process reduce meeting load without weakening relationships?
- Which parts belong in reusable discussion or poll templates?
- Is another circle ready to adopt the same practice, or does it need different rules?

Add another circle only when someone can facilitate and maintain it. A larger empty structure is less useful than one circle with a dependable decision habit.

## Recover when participation falls

If Loomio use fades, do not begin by adding more groups or templates.

1. Ask members what prevents participation.
2. Choose one current decision that should not remain in chat or wait for the next meeting.
3. Shorten and clarify the request.
4. Invite the exact people responsible and send a reminder.
5. Meet if needed, then publish the outcome in Loomio.
6. Close or archive unused spaces so the active path is obvious.

The goal is not to make every interaction happen in Loomio. The goal is a reliable path from an issue to a decision, an owner, and a reviewable record.
